This is a general educational checklist for preparing your apartment and records before move-out. It does not decide what a landlord or property manager can deduct, and it does not replace local tenant guidance or legal advice.

Before you start cleaning

  • Read the lease move-out section, including cleaning, carpet, trash, key, notice, and forwarding-address instructions.
  • Ask your property manager how final inspection works and whether there is a move-out form or preferred process.
  • Confirm elevator, loading dock, parking, trash room, and bulk-item rules before move-out day.
  • Make a list of keys, fobs, remotes, parking passes, mailbox keys, and access cards to return.

Documentation checklist

  • Take clear dated photos or video of every room before major cleaning if you want a baseline.
  • After cleaning, photograph appliances, cabinets, closets, floors, walls, windows, bathrooms, and entry areas.
  • Photograph returned keys/fobs or get a written confirmation if the property provides one.
  • Save receipts for professional cleaning, carpet cleaning, repair supplies, disposal fees, and final utilities.
  • Keep move-out emails, text messages, inspection notes, and instructions in one folder.

Common deduction categories to ask about

At a high level, deposit deductions often relate to unpaid rent or fees, cleaning beyond normal expectations, damage beyond ordinary wear, missing items or keys, or lease-specific charges. The details vary by location, lease, and facts, so use this as a question list rather than a legal conclusion.

  • Are there unpaid balances, utility charges, parking charges, or move-out fees to settle?
  • Does the building require specific cleaning, carpet cleaning, or trash-removal steps?
  • Are any keys, fobs, passes, remotes, or fixtures missing?
  • Are there repair items you should ask about before attempting DIY fixes?
