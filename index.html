Security deposit prep checklist

This checklist is meant to help you organize cleaning, documentation, and questions to ask. It cannot guarantee whether a landlord or property manager will make deductions, and it does not replace local tenant guidance or legal advice.

  • Read your lease move-out section and any building move-out instructions.
  • Ask how the final inspection works and what access, elevator, trash, or parking rules apply.
  • Take dated photos or video of each room before and after cleaning.
  • Save receipts for cleaning supplies, professional cleaning, repairs, and disposal fees.
  • Return all keys, fobs, remotes, parking passes, mailbox keys, and access cards.
  • Provide a forwarding address if your lease or property manager requests one.
  • Keep important move-out communication in writing when possible.

Move-out cost categories renters forget

Not sure what to enter? Start with rough numbers. The goal is not a perfect quote — it is to spot costs you might otherwise forget.

  • Cleaning supplies: trash bags, sponges, degreaser, mop pads, gloves, and appliance cleaner.
  • Lease-required cleaning: carpet cleaning, professional cleaning, or specific receipt requirements if your lease or building asks for them.
  • Trash and bulk removal: dump fees, junk pickup, extra bags, or hauling help for furniture you are not taking.
  • Patch or repair supplies: spackle, bulbs, replacement hardware, or paint touch-up only where allowed.
  • Missing items: replacement keys, fobs, remotes, parking passes, or mailbox keys.
  • Packing and moving: boxes, tape, labels, truck rental, mileage, fuel, movers, or helper tips.
  • Final utilities: old-unit final bills, overlap days, internet return fees, or transfer charges.
  • Pet-related cleanup: odor treatment, extra vacuuming, or lease-specific pet cleaning steps if applicable.

Room-by-room cleaning checklist

Work from low-use spaces first, then finish kitchens, bathrooms, floors, and photos close to key return.

  • Kitchen: clean appliances, counters, cabinets, sink, backsplash, floor, and inside drawers.
  • Bathroom: scrub toilet, tub/shower, sink, mirror, fan cover, cabinets, and floor edges.
  • Bedrooms: empty closets, wipe shelves, dust fixtures, remove tape, and vacuum or mop floors.
  • Living areas: dust baseboards, clean windowsills, wipe switches, and remove furniture marks where possible.
  • Floors and carpets: vacuum slowly, mop hard floors, and follow lease instructions for carpets or rugs.
  • Windows and blinds: dust blinds, wipe tracks, clean reachable glass, and report pre-existing damage if needed.
  • Entry, porch, storage: clear closets, patio items, storage cages, and assigned garage spaces.

Simple move-out timeline

  • 30 days before: check notice requirements, lease move-out instructions, elevator reservations, and mover availability.
  • 2 weeks before: declutter, donate or dispose of unwanted items, gather supplies, and schedule cleaning or repair help.
  • 1 week before: confirm inspection details, clean low-use areas, pack nonessentials, and take first documentation photos.
  • Final day: finish cleaning, take photos/video, return keys/fobs, share forwarding address if needed, and save receipts.

Deposit deduction basics, carefully worded

Security deposit rules vary by location, lease, building rules, and specific facts. At a general planning level, deductions often relate to unpaid rent or fees, cleaning beyond normal expectations, damage beyond ordinary wear, missing items or keys, or lease-specific charges.

Use the checklist to prepare questions, document condition, and keep receipts. Do not treat this site as a decision about what is legally allowed in your situation.

Moving-out FAQ

How much should I budget for moving out of an apartment?

Budget depends on your apartment size, cleaning condition, local prices, truck or mover needs, utility overlap, and lease instructions. Use the calculator above to build a rough planning number from the categories you actually expect.

What move-out costs do renters often forget?

Common forgotten costs include bulk trash removal, boxes and tape, replacement keys or fobs, carpet cleaning if required, final utility bills, elevator or parking logistics, and small repair supplies.

Should I hire a professional cleaner before moving out?

It depends on your lease, budget, time, and apartment condition. Some renters clean themselves; others hire help for kitchens, bathrooms, carpets, or a receipt if their lease or building asks for one.

What should I photograph before handing over keys?

Photograph or record each room, appliances, floors, walls, windows, closets, cabinets, keys/fobs, and any cleaning or repair work you completed. Keep copies with dates.

What can affect a security deposit return?

Possible factors include lease terms, local rules, unpaid balances, property condition, documentation, missing items, and inspection findings. This site cannot predict or guarantee a deposit outcome.

What is considered normal wear and tear?

The meaning can vary by location and situation. As a practical starting point, document the apartment condition, review your lease, and check local renter or tenant resources instead of relying on a broad definition.

Should I patch nail holes before moving out?

Check your lease or building instructions first. Some properties prefer specific repair methods or no DIY repairs, while others expect small patching.

How early should I start cleaning before move-out day?

Start low-use areas one to two weeks ahead if you can. Save daily-use kitchen, bathroom, floors, and final photos for the last stretch after furniture is out.

What receipts should I keep after moving out?

Keep receipts for professional cleaning, carpet cleaning if used, supplies, repairs, trash removal, truck or mover costs, key/fob returns, and utility final payments.

Is this checklist legal advice?

No. It is general educational planning information for renters. Check your lease, local renter resources, and official guidance for your area.

What to keep in one move-out folder

Keep your move-out records together so you can answer questions quickly after key return. Save a copy of your lease move-out instructions, inspection details, dated room photos or video, cleaning and repair receipts, utility confirmations, forwarding address notes, and key/fob return confirmation.
